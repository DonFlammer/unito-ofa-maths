## Where to start

1. Take the [entry test](sito:entry-test.html): 24 questions, about 30 minutes, without a calculator. At the end you see your result module by module, and the plan above gives more time to the modules where you did worst.
2. Choose the exam date to aim for. The degree programme recommends the **autumn sittings** (in 2025/26 they were on 24 and 28 November): the sooner you catch up on the basics, the faster you move on with your studies.
3. Decide how many hours a week you can put in, on top of lectures. If you start with serious gaps you need about 75 hours in all: sixty or so for the eight modules, the rest for the mock tests and revision. That means about 10 hours a week for 8 weeks, or 6 hours a week for 13 weeks. If some modules go well in the entry test, the plan takes time away from them.
4. Follow the modules **in order**: each module uses the ones before it (rational equations use factorisation, logarithmic inequalities use second-degree ones, and so on).

## How to study a module

> [!METODO] A module in six steps
> 1. **Read the notes** for the module with pen and paper. When you reach a worked example, cover it and try to do it on your own, then compare.
> 2. **Open the Libro (book) for the same module** on the [OFA platform](https://www.ofa.unito.it/course/view.php?id=20) (OFA: additional learning requirement, *obbligo formativo aggiuntivo*): it is the material the test is built on. It also helps you get used to the notation you will find.
> 3. **Exercises**: first those in the notes, from basic level to test level, then the “Esercizi” (exercises) PDFs on the platform, checking with the “Soluzioni” (solutions) PDFs.
> 4. **Online tests on the platform**: the “Test” of each unit and the “Test modulo” (module test). Redo it until you get through it with no mistakes.
> 5. **Quizzes and checklist** at the end of the notes. If a checklist item is not clear to you, reread that section.
> 6. **Spaced revision**: every two modules, 20 minutes on the [formula sheet](sito:formulas.html) for the previous modules.

> [!TRAPPOLA] Reading is not enough
> Rereading the notes gives you the impression that you have understood, but in the test you need to be able to do the calculations on your own, by hand and with the clock ticking. For every hour of reading, at least one hour of exercises.

## An example: eight weeks to the end of November

This is the plan the site suggests if you start at the end of September with 10 hours a week and aim for a sitting at the end of November. The plan at the top of the page recalculates it for your dates and your hours.

| Week | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | online transcript (libretto), enrolment in the OFA course, entry test; module 1 (language, sets, numbers) |
| 2 | module 2 (polynomials and factorisation); module 3, first- and second-degree equations and inequalities |
| 3 | module 3, systems; module 4, rational and radical equations and inequalities |
| 4 | module 4, absolute value; module 5, Cartesian plane, straight line and the first conics |
| 5 | end of module 5; module 6 (functions) |
| 6 | **register for the sitting** as soon as registration opens; module 7 (exponentials and logarithms); start of module 8 |
| 7 | module 8 (trigonometry); mock test 1 |
| 8 | mock tests 2–8 and targeted revision of the modules you got wrong; the day after, the test |

## Without a calculator

In the test the numbers are chosen so that the calculations can be done by hand, but you need to be quick. Train like this right from the start:

- **Fractions**: simplify before multiplying; to add, use the least common multiple, not the product of the denominators.
- **Powers and roots**: learn by heart the squares up to $20^2$, the cubes up to $10^3$, the powers of 2 up to $2^{10} = 1024$, the powers of 3 up to $3^5 = 243$.
- **Logarithms**: recognise the powers: $\log_2 32 = 5$ because $2^5 = 32$; $\log_{10} 0.001 = -3$.
- **Trigonometry**: you must know the values of sine, cosine and tangent for $0$, $\frac{\pi}{6}$, $\frac{\pi}{4}$, $\frac{\pi}{3}$, $\frac{\pi}{2}$ without thinking; the others are worked out from the unit circle.
- **Checking**: before choosing an answer, estimate the order of magnitude and substitute the result into the original equation. In multiple-choice quizzes, this is often the quickest way.

## During the 45 minutes

> [!METODO] Strategy for the test
> 1. **Read all 5 questions** (one minute): work out which ones you can do straight away.
> 2. **Start with the easiest**: scoring points early takes the anxiety away.
> 3. **Keep up the pace**: about 9 minutes per question. If after 12 minutes you are still stuck, move on to the next one and come back later.
> 4. **Two-part questions**: each part is worth 1 point. If the second is hard, at least make sure of the first.
> 5. **Numeric answers**: write the number in the form requested (integer, decimal or fraction) and double-check the sign.
> 6. **No penalties**: never leave an answer blank; if you don't know, rule out the impossible options and choose.
> 7. **Last 5 minutes**: double-check the domain conditions and the directions of the inequalities, where most points are lost.

## If you get stuck

- Reread the section of the notes and redo the worked examples before the exercises.
- Ask the first-year tutors ([tutorato.informatica@unito.it](mailto:tutorato.informatica@unito.it)) or in the first-year Telegram group: someone is often preparing for the same sitting.
- For doubts about the OFA rules: [commofa@educ.di.unito.it](mailto:commofa@educ.di.unito.it). All the contacts are on the page [Rules, dates and contacts](sito:rules.html#help-and-contacts).
