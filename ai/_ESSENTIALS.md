# Instructions for the AI reading these files

> Italian original: <https://github.com/DonFlammer/unito-ofa-matematica/blob/main/ai/istruzioni-per-ai.md>

You are the tutor of a first-year **Computer Science student at the University of Turin (academic year 2026/27)** who has to make up the **maths OFA** (additional learning requirement, *obbligo formativo aggiuntivo*): in the TOLC-S they scored less than 5/20 in the Basic Mathematics section (Matematica di base) and must pass the OFA test within the first year, otherwise they will not be able to have second-year exams recorded. Their details (test date, hours available, weak modules) are in `student.md`, if they have filled it in. The official “OFA Matematica” course, its material and the test are in Italian: `italian-glossary.md` lists the Italian terms of each module and the typical phrasings of the questions.

These files contain research that has already been done and verified: **use them as your main source instead of researching from scratch**, and point out if anything seems out of date to you (the rules can change every academic year).

## What is verified and what is not

- **Rules, test, venue, contacts** (`ofa-rules.md`): from the official sources of the degree programme and of UniTo, checked on 29 September 2026; each section cites its source.
- **Dates**: the **2026/27 test calendar had not yet been published** as of 30 September 2026 (checked again on the degree programme's requirements page). The 2025/26 dates are real data from the exam listings on Esse3 (bacheca appelli); the 2026/27 dates in the study plan are **estimates** modelled on 2025/26. Do not invent dates: refer to the degree programme's requirements page and to the Esse3 exam listings (the procedure is in `ofa-rules.md`).
- **Platform course** (`official-course.md`): structure read on the platform with a student's login; the course is in Italian. The official material is not copied here. The file contains the list of **verified typos** in the material: if the student brings you an official exercise whose solution doesn't add up, check that list first.
- **Mathematics** (modules, exercises, quizzes, entry test, mock tests): they follow the course syllabus; **every result was recomputed independently** with Python and SymPy (over 3,300 automatic checks, including all the wrong quiz options) and every graph was checked visually. Errors in wording are still possible: if you find a result that doesn't add up, recompute it and say so.
- **Not known**: whether multiple-choice questions in the real test give partial credit (in the mock tests in these files the point counts only if all the choices are right), and which specific questions the test uses, beyond the fact that they are based on all the course material.

## How to help

1. **Find out where the student is starting from**: if they have not taken the entry test, give them `entry-test.md` one question at a time, without showing the right options or the explanations, and at the end summarise the result module by module (fewer than 2 right answers out of 3 = a module to go back over).
2. **Follow the syllabus in order** (each module uses the earlier ones) and the plan in `study-plan.md`, adapted to the hours and the date in `student.md`.
3. **Explain using the notes of the right module**, with their notation, which is the course's: $\sin$, $\cos$, $\tan$, $\operatorname{cotan}$, $\ln$, $\log_a$, intervals with round and square brackets. Start from the worked examples; the “Common mistake” callouts show where people go wrong most often.
4. **Make the student do the work**: set an exercise, wait for their attempt, then correct the steps, not just the result. Hints first, then the full solution only if they ask for it.
5. **No calculator**: it is not allowed in the test. If calculations are needed, show how to do them by hand and with which tricks (simplifying before multiplying, recognising powers, estimating).
6. **Always check** domain conditions, the direction of inequalities, solutions to discard and interval endpoints: these are the mistakes that cost the most points.
7. **Simulate the test** with `mock-tests.md`: 5 questions, 45 minutes (60 with extra time), no calculator. Show the questions without the solutions, collect the answers, then mark them: 2 points per question, 1 per part in two-part questions, no penalties, pass mark 6/10. Then give the explanations, including for the right answers.
8. **Special cases** in the rules (transfers, second degrees, late enrolment, SpLD [specific learning disorders, DSA]): do not improvise, refer the student to the OFA committee (commissione OFA), commofa@educ.di.unito.it.

## How to read the files

The syntax is described in `FORMAT.md`. In short:

- `> [!DEF]`, `> [!PROP]` (rule), `> [!METODO]` (method), `> [!ESEMPIO]` (example), `> [!TRAPPOLA]` (common mistake), `> [!TEST]` (tip for the test), `> [!NOTA]` (note): callouts; on the site, definitions, rules and examples are numbered by module (“Definition 3.1”).
- `::: esercizio base|medio|test title` … `::: soluzione` … `:::`: an exercise (basic, intermediate or test level) with its solution.
- `quiz`, `quiz diagnostico` (diagnostic quiz) and `simulazione` (mock test) blocks: `D:` is the question (domanda), `+` a right option, `-` a wrong one, `N:` a numeric answer (with an optional tolerance `±`), `=` the explanation, `a)` and `b)` the parts of a question, worth 1 point each.
- `grafico` (graph) and `retta` (number line) blocks: they describe the figures on the site (plotted functions, points, intervals on the number line); they can also be read as text.
- Links `sito:page.html` (sito = site) point to the site: https://donflammer.github.io/unito-ofa-maths/page.html.

## If you need to write or correct content

1. Follow `FORMAT.md` and the structure of the existing modules: front matter, “In brief”, one section per unit of the official course, callouts, exercises at the three levels, check quizzes and checklist.
2. Write from scratch: do not copy texts, exercises or figures from the official material.
3. Recompute every result, including the wrong quiz options, with a symbolic computation tool, and do not reproduce the typos listed in `official-course.md`.
4. Check the file with `node tools/check.mjs <file>` and rebuild the site with `node tools/build.mjs` (which also regenerates `_ALL_IN_ONE.md` and `_ESSENTIALS.md`).

## In brief

- You have the **maths OFA** if you scored **less than 5 points out of 20** in the Basic Mathematics section (Matematica di base) of the TOLC-S. Your online transcript (libretto) on MyUnito shows the activity **“INT1475 OFA - MATEMATICA”**.
- To make it up you must take the **“OFA Matematica” catch-up course (corso di riallineamento)**, in Italian, on [www.ofa.unito.it](https://www.ofa.unito.it/course/view.php?id=20) and **pass the in-person exam**.
- The exam, in Italian: **5 questions** worth 2 points each, **45 minutes**, pass mark **6/10**, **no calculator**, on a computer in the **Turing Lab (Laboratorio Turing)**.
- You register for exam dates on **MyUnito**, for **only one sitting per exam period (session)**; places are limited.
- It must be passed **within the first year**: without it, you cannot have second-year exams recorded. You can, however, take first-year exams.

## Who has the OFA

Computer Science has open admission, but to enrol you must sit the **TARM** (the entry requirements test), which is taken through CISIA's **TOLC-S**. The TOLC-S has 55 questions in 6 sections (Basic Mathematics; Reasoning, comprehension, problems; Biology; Chemistry; Physics; Earth Sciences) plus 30 English questions, in 120 + 15 minutes.

For the degree programme, the test is passed with **at least 5 points out of 20 in the Basic Mathematics section**. Anyone who scores below that gets the maths OFA. There are no other thresholds.

> [!NOTA] Physics
> If you scored less than 2/10 in the Physics section, the degree programme **recommends** (but does not require) that you take the physics catch-up course, also on www.ofa.unito.it. It is not an OFA and there is no exam to pass.

Sources: degree programme regulations (Regolamento didattico) for the 2026 cohort, article 2, paragraphs 7–11; the page [Admission requirements, TARM and TOLC](https://laurea.informatica.unito.it/do/home.pl/View?doc=Requisiti_di_ammissione.html) (Requisiti di ammissione, TARM e TOLC).

## What you need to do

> [!METODO] From the transcript to the test
> 1. **Check your online transcript** on [MyUnito](https://my.unito.it): if “INT1475 OFA - MATEMATICA” is there, you have the OFA.
> 2. **Log in to [www.ofa.unito.it](https://www.ofa.unito.it)** with your UniTo SCU credentials (the same as for MyUnito). You must already be enrolled and have active student status.
> 3. **Enrol in the “OFA Matematica” course**: from the menu Corsi → Attività OFA (Courses → OFA activities), or [directly here](https://www.ofa.unito.it/course/view.php?id=20). Enrolment does not ask for a key.
> 4. **Study all the material** of the 8 modules: the exam questions are based on everything there is on the platform. The notes on this site follow the same modules; how the course is organised, and which typos were found in its material, is explained on the page [The official course](https://donflammer.github.io/unito-ofa-maths/course.html).
> 5. **Register for an exam sitting** on MyUnito, Esami (exams) section, as soon as registration opens: only one per exam period, limited places.
> 6. **Arrive on time** at the Turing Lab with an identity document.

The degree programme recommends preparing straight away and aiming for the **autumn sittings**, so that you can move on faster with your studies: a solid grounding in the basics matters for everything you will study.

## The exam

From the degree programme's official instructions (January 2026 update):

| What | How it works |
|---|---|
| Questions | 5, worth 2 points each. Some are split into two sub-questions worth 1 point each; others, more complex, are worth 2 points with no sub-questions. |
| Formats | one choice out of four, multiple choice, true/false, numeric answer |
| Pass mark | 6 points out of 10 |
| Penalties | none for wrong answers: it pays to answer everything |
| Time | 45 minutes (1 hour with the extra time for SpLD [specific learning disorders, DSA] or disability) |
| Calculator | not allowed, whether scientific or basic (unless granted on a request made in time for SpLD or disability) |
| Scratch paper | one sheet handed out by the invigilators, collected at the end |
| Where | Turing Lab, on a computer, on the exam platform |
| Login | UniTo SCU credentials; **you cannot log in with SPID** (the Italian public digital identity) |
| What to bring | an identity document |
| Syllabus | all the material of the “OFA Matematica” course on the platform |

> [!TEST] What this means for how you study
> With 45 minutes for 5 questions you have about 9 minutes per question, without a calculator. The questions are real exercises (an inequality, a system, a logarithm, a trigonometric equation…), not definitions learnt by heart. Practise doing the calculations by hand and against the clock: the [mock tests](https://donflammer.github.io/unito-ofa-maths/mock-tests.html) have the same format. The questions are in Italian, like the whole course and its material: the page [Italian for the OFA test](https://donflammer.github.io/unito-ofa-maths/glossary.html) lists the Italian words and phrasings you will meet.

## Where

The test takes place in the **Turing Lab** of the **Department of Computer Science (Dipartimento di Informatica)**, **via Pessinetto 12, Turin**, on the raised ground floor (the same building as lecture rooms A and B). On Esse3 the room appears as “Lab Turing Informatica”. Map: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/search?query=Via%20Pessinetto%2012%2C%20Torino).

## SpLD and disability

If you have a disability or an SpLD, when you register for the exam date you follow the same procedure as for other exams: the University's guidelines are on the page [Support for students with SpLD](https://www.unito.it/servizi/lo-studio/studenti-e-studentesse-con-disturbi-specifici-di-apprendimento-dsa/supporto) (Supporto per studenti con DSA) and the documentation must be sent to the lecturers of the sitting (list on the [degree programme's lecturers page](https://laurea.informatica.unito.it/do/docenti.pl/Search?format=8&rs=1;title=Ricevimento%20studenti&max=5000)). The extra time is one third: **1 hour instead of 45 minutes**. A calculator can be allowed only for those who requested it within the deadlines.

## If you don't pass

- You try again in the **next exam period** (one sitting per exam period).
- The OFA must be passed **within the first year of your programme**. If you don't manage it, **you cannot have second-year exams recorded** until you pass it.
- You can take **first-year exams** even while the OFA is still outstanding.

> [!PROP] The rule for second-year exams
> The 2026/27 Degree Programme Guide (Guida del corso di laurea) says that exams of a year after the first can be recorded only after recording **at least 21 CFU (ECTS credits) of first-year exams** and **having passed the maths OFA** (for those who did not reach the TOLC-S threshold).

The degree programme regulations (article 9) set the same 21 CFU threshold; the OFA is added to it for those who have one.

## Frequently asked questions

**I scored 5/20 or more in Basic Mathematics: do I need to do anything?** No, you don't have the OFA.

**Can I make up the OFA by passing a first-year maths exam?** The official instructions give only one way: the course on the platform and its exam. If your case is special (for example you have transferred or come from another degree), ask the OFA committee.

**Is the course on the platform compulsory?** The degree programme “requires attendance and passing” of the catch-up course, i.e. studying its material and passing its exam. In any case the questions are based on that material: study all of it.

**Can I register for two sittings in the same exam period?** No: one and only one.

**Is the test on paper?** No, on a computer on the exam platform, with your SCU credentials. You only get one sheet of scratch paper.

**What happens with questions split into two parts?** Each part is worth 1 point: you can get one even if you get the other wrong.

## In brief

- The catch-up course (corso di riallineamento) is called **“OFA Matematica”** (OFA: additional learning requirement, *obbligo formativo aggiuntivo*) and is on [www.ofa.unito.it](https://www.ofa.unito.it/course/view.php?id=20) (course number 20). You log in with your UniTo SCU credentials and enrol yourself, with no enrolment key.
- It has **8 modules**, divided into numbered **units** (1.1, 1.2, 2.1, …). For each unit there is a **Libro** (book), almost always an interactive **“Esplora”** (explore) worksheet, often a page of **Applicazioni** (applications), **Esercizi** and **Soluzioni** (exercises and solutions) as PDFs and an online **Test**; at the end of each module there is an **end-of-module test**.
- The test questions are based on **all the course material**: the notes in this guide follow the same modules and the same units, but they do not replace it.
- The material contains a few **typos**: you will find them at the bottom of this page, already verified. The notes have the correct version.

## The activities of each unit

| Activity | What it is | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Libro | the theory of the unit, divided into chapters, with worked examples | read it after the notes on the same topic, to get used to the course notation |
| Esplora | an interactive Maple worksheet, with graphs and calculations on the topic of the unit | use it to see what happens when a coefficient changes |
| Applicazioni | a page or a PDF with a real-world problem solved with the maths of the unit | read it as an extra worked example |
| Esercizi and Soluzioni | two PDFs: first the exercises, then the solutions (often just the result) | do them all by hand and check at the end |
| Test | an online test (Maple T.A.) on the unit; at the end of the module there is the end-of-module test | redo it until you get through it with no mistakes |

The book shows the formulas as images: if a formula is hard to read, zoom in on the page.

## The course notation

The notes use the same notation, so there are no surprises in the test:

- trigonometric functions $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $\tan x$ and $\operatorname{cotan} x$; natural logarithm $\ln x$, logarithm to base $a$ $\log_a x$;
- intervals with round brackets (endpoint excluded) and square brackets (endpoint included): $(-2, 3]$, $(-\infty, 1)$;
- in solutions: $\forall x \in \R$ (every real number), $\nexists x \in \R$ (no solution), $\vee$ for “or” (oppure); “t.c.” for “such that” (tale che);
- in trigonometry exercises the general solutions are written with $k \in \Z$, for example $x = \frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$.

## Typos in the material

Recalculating the course exercises to write the notes turned up a few typos. Each one was double-checked with symbolic computation and **compared with the original page or PDF** on 30 September 2026. The notes have the correct version. The list only includes the verified typos and is not a complete review of the material; in module 3 none were found. If you find others you can report them to the [platform helpdesk](https://www.ofa.unito.it/helpdesk/index.php?t=tcre).

### Module 1

- **Libro 1.1**, proof by contradiction that if $n^2$ is even then $n$ is even: with $n = 2p + 1$ we get $n^2 = 4p^2 + 4p + 1$, not $4p^2 + 2p + 1$. The conclusion ($n^2$ is odd) is still correct.
- **Libro 1.1**, example on how to state a theorem: two real numbers with the same absolute value are equal **or** opposite ($a = b$ or $a = -b$), not “equal and opposite” (uguali ed opposti).
- **Libro 1.2**, extension from $\Z$ to $\Q$: the equation $ax = b$ has the solution $x = \frac{b}{a}$ when $a \ne 0$; the book writes $b \ne 0$.
- **Soluzioni 1.1, exercise 2**, items iv and v: the negation of “all children are more than 6 years old” is “at least one child is **at most** 6 years old”, not “less than 6 years old” (a child of exactly 6 makes the original sentence false). In the same way, the negation of “there is a building with more than 10 floors” is “every building has at most 10 floors”.
- **Soluzioni 1.2, exercise 1**: the largest four-digit number in which units plus tens equals hundreds plus thousands is $9999$ ($9 + 9 = 9 + 9$), not $9054$. Even requiring all the digits to be different, there are numbers larger than $9054$, for example $9687$.
- **Soluzioni 1.2, exercise 5**: $\frac{27}{8}$ is not equal to $\frac{9}{2}$; an equivalent fraction is $\frac{27}{6}$.

### Module 2

- **Libro 2.1**, taking out the common factor: $15x^3 + 3x^2 - 6x = 3x(5x^2 + x - 2)$, not $3x(5x^3 + x^2 - 2)$.
- **Soluzioni 2.1, exercise 3**: $(2x^3 - 10x^2 + 3x - 15) : (x - 5)$ gives the quotient $2x^2 + 3$ (remainder $0$), not $2x^3 + 3$.
- **Soluzioni 2.1, exercise 6**: $8x^3 + 36x^2 + 54x + 27 = (2x + 3)^3$, not $(2x + 3)^2$: the text itself talks about the cube of a binomial.

### Module 4

- **Libro 4.3, example 4** ($|x + 1| + |x - 2| \le 5$): the first system gives $-2 \le x < -1$, not $-2 \le x < 1$. The final result, $-2 \le x \le 3$, is correct.
- **Soluzioni 4.2, exercise 6** ($\sqrt{7x + 2} + \sqrt{16 - x^2} > -5$): the solution is the domain, $-\frac{2}{7} \le x \le 4$, not “for every real $x$”. The reasoning on the sheet (a sum of square roots is never negative) is correct, but it only holds where the roots exist.

### Module 5

- **Libro 5.2**, circle, tangent line to $x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 4y = 0$ at the point $P_0 = (-6, 4)$: the line through $P_0$ with slope $\frac{3}{2}$ is written $y - 4 = \frac{3}{2}(x + 6)$. The book writes $y - 2 = \frac{3}{2}(x + 3)$, which passes through the centre $(-3, 2)$; the final result, $y = \frac{3}{2}x + 13$, is correct.

### Module 6

- **Libro 6.1**, translations: $\cos\left(\frac{x}{2} + 1\right)$ is obtained from $\cos\frac{x}{2}$ by a translation of **2** units to the left, not one, because $\cos\left(\frac{x}{2} + 1\right) = \cos\frac{x + 2}{2}$. In the same way, $\cos\left(\frac{x}{2} - 1\right)$ is shifted 2 units to the right. The vertical shifts ($\pm 1$) are correct.
- **Soluzioni 6.1, exercise 1**: the domain of $f(x) = \sqrt{2x^2 - x - 3}$ is $x \le -1$ or $x \ge \frac{3}{2}$, not $x \le 1$ or $x \ge \frac{3}{2}$.

### Module 7

- **Soluzioni 7.2, exercise 6** ($\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{x^2 - 1} < 9^{2x}$): the solution is $x < -2 - \sqrt{5}$ or $x > -2 + \sqrt{5}$. The sheet writes $x < -1 - \sqrt{5}$ in the first part; the second is correct.

### Module 8

- **Libro 8.2**, inequalities, example 2 ($\cos 2x < 1$ in $[0, 2\pi]$): $\cos 2x = 1$ when $2x$ equals $0$, $2\pi$ or $4\pi$, i.e. for $x = 0$, $x = \pi$ and $x = 2\pi$. The solution is $0 < x < \pi$ or $\pi < x < 2\pi$; the book only excludes $0$ and $2\pi$.
- **Soluzioni 8.2, exercise 10** ($\tan x \ge 1$ in $[0, 2\pi]$): the solution is $\frac{\pi}{4} \le x < \frac{\pi}{2}$ or $\frac{5}{4}\pi \le x < \frac{3}{2}\pi$. The one on the sheet ($0 \le x < \frac{\pi}{2}$, $\frac{3}{4}\pi \le x < \frac{3}{2}\pi$, $\frac{7}{4}\pi \le x \le 2\pi$) is the solution of $\tan x \ge -1$.

## Where to start

1. Take the [entry test](https://donflammer.github.io/unito-ofa-maths/entry-test.html): 24 questions, about 30 minutes, without a calculator. At the end you see your result module by module, and the plan above gives more time to the modules where you did worst.
2. Choose the exam date to aim for. The degree programme recommends the **autumn sittings** (in 2025/26 they were on 24 and 28 November): the sooner you catch up on the basics, the faster you move on with your studies.
3. Decide how many hours a week you can put in, on top of lectures. If you start with serious gaps you need about 75 hours in all: sixty or so for the eight modules, the rest for the mock tests and revision. That means about 10 hours a week for 8 weeks, or 6 hours a week for 13 weeks. If some modules go well in the entry test, the plan takes time away from them.
4. Follow the modules **in order**: each module uses the ones before it (rational equations use factorisation, logarithmic inequalities use second-degree ones, and so on).

## How to study a module

> [!METODO] A module in six steps
> 1. **Read the notes** for the module with pen and paper. When you reach a worked example, cover it and try to do it on your own, then compare.
> 2. **Open the Libro (book) for the same module** on the [OFA platform](https://www.ofa.unito.it/course/view.php?id=20) (OFA: additional learning requirement, *obbligo formativo aggiuntivo*): it is the material the test is built on. It also helps you get used to the notation you will find.
> 3. **Exercises**: first those in the notes, from basic level to test level, then the “Esercizi” (exercises) PDFs on the platform, checking with the “Soluzioni” (solutions) PDFs.
> 4. **Online tests on the platform**: the “Test” of each unit and the “Test modulo” (module test). Redo it until you get through it with no mistakes.
> 5. **Quizzes and checklist** at the end of the notes. If a checklist item is not clear to you, reread that section.
> 6. **Spaced revision**: every two modules, 20 minutes on the [formula sheet](https://donflammer.github.io/unito-ofa-maths/formulas.html) for the previous modules.

> [!TRAPPOLA] Reading is not enough
> Rereading the notes gives you the impression that you have understood, but in the test you need to be able to do the calculations on your own, by hand and with the clock ticking. For every hour of reading, at least one hour of exercises.

## Without a calculator

In the test the numbers are chosen so that the calculations can be done by hand, but you need to be quick. Train like this right from the start:

- **Fractions**: simplify before multiplying; to add, use the least common multiple, not the product of the denominators.
- **Powers and roots**: learn by heart the squares up to $20^2$, the cubes up to $10^3$, the powers of 2 up to $2^{10} = 1024$, the powers of 3 up to $3^5 = 243$.
- **Logarithms**: recognise the powers: $\log_2 32 = 5$ because $2^5 = 32$; $\log_{10} 0.001 = -3$.
- **Trigonometry**: you must know the values of sine, cosine and tangent for $0$, $\frac{\pi}{6}$, $\frac{\pi}{4}$, $\frac{\pi}{3}$, $\frac{\pi}{2}$ without thinking; the others are worked out from the unit circle.
- **Checking**: before choosing an answer, estimate the order of magnitude and substitute the result into the original equation. In multiple-choice quizzes, this is often the quickest way.

## During the 45 minutes

> [!METODO] Strategy for the test
> 1. **Read all 5 questions** (one minute): work out which ones you can do straight away.
> 2. **Start with the easiest**: scoring points early takes the anxiety away.
> 3. **Keep up the pace**: about 9 minutes per question. If after 12 minutes you are still stuck, move on to the next one and come back later.
> 4. **Two-part questions**: each part is worth 1 point. If the second is hard, at least make sure of the first.
> 5. **Numeric answers**: write the number in the form requested (integer, decimal or fraction) and double-check the sign.
> 6. **No penalties**: never leave an answer blank; if you don't know, rule out the impossible options and choose.
> 7. **Last 5 minutes**: double-check the domain conditions and the directions of the inequalities, where most points are lost.

## If you get stuck

- Reread the section of the notes and redo the worked examples before the exercises.
- Ask the first-year tutors ([tutorato.informatica@unito.it](mailto:tutorato.informatica@unito.it)) or in the first-year Telegram group: someone is often preparing for the same sitting.
- For doubts about the OFA rules: [commofa@educ.di.unito.it](mailto:commofa@educ.di.unito.it). All the contacts are on the page [Rules, dates and contacts](https://donflammer.github.io/unito-ofa-maths/rules.html#help-and-contacts).
